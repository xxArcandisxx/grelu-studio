## **4. The one result I would investigate further**

Your **average precision of 0.320** is the least straightforward result.

It does **not** mean that the model is 32% accurate. Average precision summarizes how well the model retrieves positive variants across different ranking thresholds.

Its interpretation depends heavily on the proportion of positive variants. For random ranking, expected average precision is approximately the positive-class prevalence.&#x20;

## **5. What is still missing?**

Before calling the model scientifically validated, I'd check:

1. **Data leakage:** Were the 500 regions genuinely held out, with no overlapping or near-duplicate training regions?
2. **Positive variant count:** How many of the 1,000 variants were labeled positive?
3. **Baseline fairness:** Were your model and gkmSVM evaluated on exactly the same variants, labels, and metrics?
4. **Uncertainty:** Do the improvements remain when you repeat testing or calculate confidence intervals?
5. **DNase error baseline:** How does MSE 0.571 compare against simply predicting the mean measured DNase activity? Shuffling is a useful sanity check, but not the strongest baseline.
